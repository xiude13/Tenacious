Privacy Policy

Tenacious

Last updated: September 9, 2026 · Applies to the Tenacious Android app (com.tenacious.app)

In short: Tenacious stores your workout plans, history, and settings only on your own device. The one thing it sends anywhere is your GPS route during an active Cardio session — and that goes to OpenStreetMap, just to draw the map, not to us. We don't run any servers, don't have user accounts, and don't sell or share your data with anyone.

What Tenacious stores, and where

Everything you enter into Tenacious — workout plans, exercise history, completed sessions, cardio routes, and your app settings — is stored locally on your device only, using Android's standard app storage. None of it is uploaded to us, because we don't operate any server that could receive it.

Android's own device backup service may include this local data in your regular Google account backup, the same way it backs up most apps' data — that's a device-level setting, not something Tenacious controls or initiates itself.

Location

Tenacious asks for location access only to record a GPS route during an active Cardio (run/cycle tracking) session. Specifically:

  • Location is only read while you have a cardio session actively running or paused on screen — never in the background, and never outside that feature.
  • The recorded route, distance, and pace are saved locally as part of that session's history, in the same on-device storage as everything else.
  • To draw the live map during a session, Tenacious requests map imagery from OpenStreetMap's tile servers for the area you're currently near. That request necessarily reveals your approximate location (and IP address) to OpenStreetMap's infrastructure, the same way it would to any map provider — see osmfoundation.org/wiki/Privacy_Policy for their policy. We don't add any tracking of our own on top of this.
  • You can deny or later revoke location permission in your device's app settings at any time; Cardio's GPS tracking simply won't work until it's granted again.

Notifications

Tenacious can show two kinds of notifications, both generated entirely on your device:

  • Scheduled reminders for workouts you've set a reminder time for.
  • A live status notification during an active workout or cardio session, showing your current progress — this is also what lets a paired smartwatch mirror that status via Android's own Wear OS notification bridging.

These are local notifications scheduled by the app itself, not push notifications from a server — no notification content ever leaves your device.

Sharing a workout plan

The Share feature hands a plan file to whichever app you choose from your device's own share sheet (Messages, email, WhatsApp, etc.) — that's a direct action you take, not something Tenacious transmits on its own. If someone shares a plan file to you and you open it, Tenacious reads that file locally to show you an import confirmation; it isn't uploaded anywhere either.

What we don't do

  • No user accounts, sign-in, or personally-identifying information collected by us.
  • No analytics or crash-reporting SDKs.
  • No advertising or ad SDKs.
  • No selling, renting, or sharing your data with third parties, ever.

Requesting data deletion

Because Tenacious doesn't operate any server or account system, there's nothing on our end to send a deletion request to — everything is stored only on your own device, so deleting it is entirely in your control, immediately, with no waiting period:

  • To delete your workout plans and history, but keep the app: open Tenacious, go to Settings, and tap "Clear all data." This immediately and permanently deletes every saved plan and your entire workout/cardio history.
  • To delete everything, including your settings: uninstall Tenacious from your device. This removes all remaining local app data (subject to the Android device-backup note above, which is a device-level setting outside the app's control).

What's deleted: workout plans, exercise history, completed sessions, cardio routes, and app settings. There's no server-side copy of any of it to separately delete, and no retention period — it's gone as soon as you take either action above.

Children's privacy

Tenacious is a general-audience fitness utility, not directed at children, and we don't knowingly collect data from children. Since the app doesn't collect data from us in the first place, this is largely academic — but if you believe a child has used the app and you have a concern, contact us at the address below.
